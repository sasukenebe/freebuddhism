Buddhist Texts

The Theravāda tradition preserves its primary texts in the Pāli Tipiṭaka, or “three baskets”: discipline, discourses, and systematic teachings.

Pāli Tipiṭaka

Follow a linked name to read its texts. Use the arrow beside any name to reveal its description.

  • Tipiṭaka (Three Baskets)

    The Theravāda Pali canon: monastic discipline, discourses, and systematic teachings.

    • Vinaya Piṭaka (Monastic Discipline) — 5 books

      Rules and procedures for the monastic community.

      • Sutta Vibhaṅga (Analysis of the Rules)

        Monastic training rules with explanations and cases.

        • Mahā Vibhaṅga (Monks’ Rules)

          The monks’ training rules and their analysis.

        • Bhikkhunī Vibhaṅga (Nuns’ Rules)

          The nuns’ training rules and their analysis.

      • Khandhaka (Chapters)

        Procedures and communal life in the monastic community.

        • Mahāvagga (Great Chapter)

          Chapters covering ordination and other monastic procedures.

        • Cullavagga (Lesser Chapter)

          Further chapters on monastic procedures and communal life.

      • Parivāra (Compendium)

        A review and classification of the monastic rules.

    • Sutta Piṭaka (Discourses) — 5 collections

      Discourses and other works preserved in five collections.

      • Dīgha Nikāya (Long Discourses)

        Long discourses covering teachings, practice, and discussions.

      • Majjhima Nikāya (Middle-Length Discourses)

        Middle-length discourses on teachings and practice.

      • Saṁyutta Nikāya (Connected Discourses)

        Discourses grouped by topic or by the people involved.

      • Aṅguttara Nikāya (Numbered Discourses)

        Discourses arranged around numbered sets of teachings.

      • Khuddaka Nikāya (Minor Collection) — 15 books

        A varied collection of short texts, verses, and other works; its extent varies by edition.

        • Khuddakapāṭha (Short Readings)

          Short passages for recitation and basic instruction.

        • Dhammapada (Verses of the Dhamma)

          Verses on conduct, practice, and wisdom.

        • Udāna (Inspired Sayings)

          Inspired utterances with accounts of their occasions.

        • Itivuttaka (Thus It Was Said)

          Short teachings combining prose and verse.

        • Suttanipāta (Collection of Discourses)

          Discourses largely in verse.

        • Vimānavatthu (Heavenly Mansions)

          Verses about heavenly rebirth and the actions leading to it.

        • Petavatthu (Stories of Departed Spirits)

          Verses about departed beings and the consequences of actions.

        • Theragāthā (Verses of the Senior Monks)

          Verses attributed to early Buddhist monks.

        • Therīgāthā (Verses of the Senior Nuns)

          Verses attributed to early Buddhist nuns.

        • Jātaka (Birth Stories)

          Canonical verses about the Buddha’s previous lives, preserved with their traditional commentary stories in a complete six-volume English translation.

        • Niddesa (Exposition)

          Explanations of parts of the Suttanipāta.

        • Paṭisambhidāmagga (Path of Discrimination)

          Analyses of knowledge and the path of practice.

        • Apadāna (Accounts of Past Deeds)

          Verse accounts of past deeds associated with monks and nuns.

        • Buddhavaṃsa (Chronicle of Buddhas)

          Accounts of earlier Buddhas and the bodhisatta’s aspirations.

        • Cariyāpiṭaka (Basket of Conduct)

          Verse accounts illustrating the bodhisatta’s conduct.

        • Edition-specific works (Present in some Theravāda editions)

          These additional works are not part of the standard 15-book Khuddaka list in every Theravāda edition.

          • Milindapañha (Questions of King Milinda)

            A dialogue text included in some Theravāda editions; the separate SuttaCentral/Kelly segment alignment remains incomplete.

          • Nettipakaraṇa (The Guide)

            A work on methods for interpreting and explaining the Buddha’s teaching; included in some Theravāda editions.

          • Peṭakopadesa (The Piṭaka-Disclosure)

            A work on interpreting and organizing the Buddha’s teaching; included in some Theravāda editions.

    • Abhidhamma Piṭaka (Systematic Teachings) — 7 books

      Systematic analyses of phenomena and their relationships.

      • Dhammasaṅgaṇī (Enumeration of Phenomena)

        An enumeration and classification of phenomena.

      • Vibhaṅga (Book of Analysis)

        Analyses of principal categories of the teachings.

      • Dhātukathā (Discussion of Elements)

        An examination of relationships between categories of phenomena.

      • Puggalapaññatti (Designation of Persons)

        Classifications of types of persons.

      • Kathāvatthu (Points of Controversy)

        Debates about interpretations of Buddhist doctrine.

      • Yamaka (Pairs)

        Paired questions examining doctrinal categories.

      • Paṭṭhāna (Conditional Relations)

        An extensive analysis of conditional relationships.
