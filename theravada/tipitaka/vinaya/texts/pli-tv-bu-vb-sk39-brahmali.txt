Theravāda Collection on Monastic Law
The Great Analysis
The chapter on training
The subchapter on respectfully
Making an overlarge mouthful
Origin story
At one time the Buddha was staying at Sāvatthī in the Jeta Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Monastery. At that time the monks from the group of six were eating, making large mouthfuls. …
Final ruling
“‘I will not make mouthfuls that are too large,’ this is how you should train.”
One should not make mouthfuls that are too large. If a monk, out of disrespect, makes a large mouthful, he commits an offense of wrong conduct.
Non-offenses
There is no offense: if it is unintentional; if he is not mindful; if he does not know; if he is sick; if it is fresh food; if it is any kind of fruit; if it is a non-bean curry; if there is an emergency; if he is insane; if he is the first offender.
The ninth training rule is finished.
